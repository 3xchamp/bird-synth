Great Blue Heron Ardea herodias

A syrinx at its airiest — this bird barely has a pitch to track. What was measured from one reference recording is the noise itself: 13 call bursts, their lengths, gaps, loudness arc, and the exact spectral shape of each burst type. The instrument below is shaped breath — filtered noise wearing the measured spectra, with a low tonal core only where the tracker could prove one exists. Where pitch could not be proven, the page says so instead of inventing it.

Source. Modeled from xeno-canto recording XC698523 — Great Blue Heron, recorded by Thomas G. Graves, Morro Bay, San Luis Obispo County, California, 2021-12-26 · CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. A parabolic-dish recording of a single bird flying overhead “snarling” as it landed; a distant Marbled Godwit shares the file and is not modeled. Jimmy picked this recording himself. No audio from the recording is played or reused; the synth re-creates measured burst spectra and envelopes with filtered noise and oscillators.

Method, briefly. Bursts were segmented on the recording’s 200 Hz–8 kHz envelope and measured frame by frame: duration, gaps, peak level, amplitude envelope, energy in six bands (200 Hz–8 kHz) and spectral centroid. A pitch tracker was then allowed to speak only where its track was stable and prominent — it proved a tonal peak near 0.9 kHz in 8 of the 13 bursts (5 of 6 croaks, 2 of 4 snarls, 1 of 3 chips); in the rest, the squawk is honestly pitchless and the model adds no tone there beyond each type’s measured tonal share. The noise runs on one fixed path — a single looped noise source through six fixed band filters into one envelope gain — shaped only by scheduled envelopes, never reconnected per burst (the defect that sank the first cardinal build is designed out), and a compressor guards the output. The whole file was scanned for aircraft/vehicle rumble: a steady low-frequency bay ambience is present, but no swell rises above it — the file is clean. The Bird⇄Electronic slider continuously interpolates timing (toward a 112 BPM grid), the tonal core (toward an A-major-pentatonic grid, growing saw + FM edge), and the noise bands (narrowing until the squawk reads as pitched pulses).
