Great Horned Owl Bubo virginianus

A syrinx, modeled — not sampled. Every pitch, hoot length and pause in this instrument was measured from one reference recording: 8 hoots in 2 territorial series, pitch-tracked frame by frame. The bird sings one note — a fundamental at ~323 Hz, nearly pure (2nd harmonic ≈ 2–3%, 3rd under 1.5%) — and all of its music is rhythm and weight: a short knock, a long breath, the long hoot, the closer. Where the model simplifies, the notes at the foot of the page say so.

The four hoots — hear each one alone

Source. Modeled from xeno-canto recording XC11638 — Great Horned Owl, recorded by Nathan Pieplow, Boulder, Colorado, 2007-03-16, before dawn · CC BY-NC-ND 2.5. Jimmy picked this recording himself; XC grades it A and lists no background species. The license’s NoDerivs term is honored the same way as in the rest of the engine: nothing from the recording is played, looped or redistributed — only measurements (pitch tracks, durations, harmonic ratios) were taken from it, and the synth re-creates those measurements with oscillators.

Method, briefly. Hoots were segmented on the recording’s 150–900 Hz band envelope (thresholds set from this file’s measured in-band floor: median −30 dB between series, hoot peaks near −2 dB) and pitch-tracked by STFT peak-picking at 10.8 Hz resolution. The recording holds exactly two series of four hoots, near-identical — durations at each position agree within 5 ms — so every position template below is the median of just two performances, and the 27.5 s pause between series was observed exactly once. The whole file was also scanned for aircraft/vehicle rumble: the only sustained swells in either the low band or the full band are the two hoot series themselves — the scan verdict is clean (BUILD-owl.md has the numbers). The hoots are rendered from one voice (they never overlap); a very faint breath of filtered air rides under each hoot on a single fixed path whose gain is shaped per note and returns to zero between notes — at 5% of the note’s own level, because these hoots are almost pure tone. The Bird⇄Electronic slider continuously interpolates pitch (toward an A-major-pentatonic grid), timing (toward 112 BPM), and timbre (sine → saw + FM) — it is one instrument being pulled across a line, not two presets.
