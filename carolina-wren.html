Carolina Wren Thryothorus ludovicianus

A syrinx, modeled — not sampled. Every pitch curve, syllable length and pause in this instrument was measured from one reference recording: 70 syllables in 6 song bouts, pitch-tracked frame by frame. The wren's song is a repeated motif of short chips and long ringing syllables, blasted again and again — the generator below replays the six measured bout sequences themselves, with timing drawn from the measured transitions. Where the model simplifies, the notes at the foot of the page say so.

Modeled from xeno-canto recording XC364592 — Carolina Wren, recorded by Brian Murphy, Schenck Forest, Wake County, North Carolina, US, 2017-04-14 · Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial-ShareAlike 4.0 · XC grade A · background species: none (grade, license and background verified on the recording page 2026-11-12; the page was bot-gated on build night, 2026-11-11). No audio from the recording is played or reused; the synth re-creates measured pitch contours with oscillators. The recording's last ~8 seconds carry a low-frequency swell (a different, low loud vocalisation) and were excluded from every measurement; only 0–28.4 s was modeled.

Method, briefly. Syllables were segmented on the recording's band envelope and pitch-tracked by STFT peak-picking (1.2–9.5 kHz). Each phrase type below is the median of its measured instances — contour, duration range and 2nd/3rd harmonic levels included. A faint breath of filtered air rides under each syllable on a single fixed path whose gain is shaped per syllable and returns to zero between syllables. The Bird⇄Electronic slider continuously interpolates pitch (toward an A-major-pentatonic grid), timing (toward 112 BPM), and timbre (sine → saw + FM) — it is one instrument being pulled across a line, not two presets. Full method and caveats: BUILD-carolina-wren.md.
