Bird-synth engine · Song Sparrow

Song Sparrow Melospiza melodia

A syrinx, modeled — not sampled. Every pitch curve, note length and pause in this instrument was measured from one reference recording: 63 notes, 4 complete songs, pitch-tracked frame by frame. This bird's problem is structure: a pair of clear intro whistles, a half-second buzz, then a rapid trill series at a measured ~11 notes a second, resolving on a high terminal note. Nothing here was invented to “sound bird-like”; where the model simplifies, the notes at the foot of the page say so.

Source. Modeled from xeno-canto recording XC1005250 — Song Sparrow, recorded by Ryan Douglas, Damascus, Montgomery County, Maryland, 2025-06-08 · Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 · XC grade A (verified on the live recording page 2026-11-12). Background species on the recording page: none. By measured phrase-to-floor contrast (gap floor −45.2 dB RMS vs phrases −17.9 dB) it was the best-documented clean candidate of the set vetted for this build. No audio from the recording is played or reused; the synth re-creates measured pitch contours with oscillators. No recording audio is used anywhere in this page.

Method, briefly. Notes were segmented on the recording’s band envelope and pitch-tracked by STFT peak-picking (1.5–9.5 kHz). Each phrase type below is the median of its measured instances — contour, duration range and second-harmonic level included. The whole file was scanned for aircraft/vehicle rumble: no low-frequency (60–500 Hz) swell exists in this file (see BUILD-song-sparrow.md). A faint breath of filtered air rides under each note on a single fixed path whose gain is shaped per note and returns to zero between notes. The Bird⇄Electronic slider continuously interpolates pitch (toward an A-major-pentatonic grid), timing (toward 112 BPM), and timbre (sine → saw + FM) — it is one instrument being pulled across a line, not two presets. Full method and caveats: BUILD-song-sparrow.md.
